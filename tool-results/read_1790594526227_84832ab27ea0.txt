    16→ Abstract. Sitting at the core of folding-based recursion, multi-instance
    17→ accumulation still forces the recursive verifier to combine ℓ committed ob-
    18→ jects, incurring Θ(ℓ) circuit-expensive commitment random linear com-
    19→ binations (CRCs). Here ℓ denotes the number of instances accumulated
    20→ per step, and a CRC is an in-circuit random linear combination of com-
    21→ mitments. Eliminating the linear-in-ℓ CRC overhead is the central chal-
    22→ lenge, and we address it with Quasar, reducing in-circuit CRCs from Θ(ℓ)
    23→ to O(1) per step, while the unavoidable cost of reading and processing
    24→ public inputs remains linear as usual. Realizing constant-commitment
    25→ accumulation, Quasar’s multi-cast reduction commits to a union poly-
    26→ nomial and verifies a random partial evaluation, yielding a reduced in-
    27→ stance with O(1) commitments. Moreover, composing multi-cast with
    28→ a standard 2-to-1 folding reduction yields a multi-instance accumula-
    29→ tion scheme whose verifier uses O(log ℓ) field work and O(1) CRCs per
    30→ step. Across instantiations with suitable polynomial commitments, our
    31→ instantiation supports linear-time accumulation provers, plausible post-
    32→ quantum security, and parallelizable proving at each step. Not only does
    33→ Quasar keep CRCs constant in ℓ, it also yields a multi-instance incre-
    34→ mentally verifiable computation with lower recursion overhead.

    39→1 Introduction
    40→
    41→Succinct Non-interactive ARguments of Knowledge (SNARKs) enable succinct
    42→verification of computations across applications, including zero-knowledge vir-
    43→tual machines [2,46], verifiable databases [58,59], and verifiable inference [45,53].
    44→In a monolithic SNARK [50], the prover records the full execution trace and
    45→proves correctness end-to-end using a general-purpose proof system [37,9,33].
    46→As computations scale, monolithic proving remains costly despite substantial
    47→prover-side optimizations [6,50,13,35,34]. For example, proving a zkVM execu-
    48→tion, e.g., EVM or RISC-V [38,39] expressed as an arithmetic circuit with 226
    49→gates can require around 1 hour of proving time [24] and more than 200 GB of
    50→memory [54]. These costs motivate recursive and incremental approaches that
    51→avoid materializing the full trace at once, but they introduce a separate per-step
    52→verification bottleneck addressed by this work.
    53→
    54→IVC and PCD. A standard alternative is to partition a large computation into
    55→smaller chunks and represent each chunk as a predicate. The prover produces a
    56→proof for each predicate instance and then recursively “squeezes” these proofs
    57→into a single one. This avoids holding the full trace at once and enables incre-
    58→mental progress. The sequential setting is incrementally verifiable computation
    59→(IVC) [55], and its generalization to directed acyclic graphs is proof-carrying data
    60→(PCD) [26]. Compared to monolithic SNARKs, IVC/PCD reduces peak mem-
    61→ory, supports streaming proof production, and can enable prover parallelism in
    62→several constructions (discussed below).
    63→
    64→Accumulation and folding schemes. Traditional IVC/PCD via recursive compo-
    65→sition invokes a SNARK at each step to verify the previous step’s proof [10,9,28],
    66→which requires embedding a full SNARK verifier into the next-step proving cir-
    67→cuit and is typically impractical [28,25]. Accumulation (or folding) schemes in-
    68→stead aggregate per-step proofs into a succinct accumulator [14,19,18,12,42], so
    69→the recursive circuit verifies only an accumulation step. A final decider checks
    70→the terminal accumulator in time independent of the number of steps, enabling
    71→practical recursion.
    72→
    73→1.1 Problem Statements
    74→While IVC/PCD improves memory scalability, the per-step recursive circuit can
    75→still dominate cost. In Nova-style frameworks [42,43,41], the per-step circuit typ-
    76→ically splits into two components.
    77→• The trace for computing a new predicate input (the computation overhead ).
    78→• The trace for verifying the accumulation between the new proof and the run-
    79→ ning accumulator (the recursion overhead )4 .
    80→Empirically, for R1CS, the recursion overhead for a single accumulation can
    81→consume around 10,000 constraints [44], which is 2–3× larger than the compu-
    82→tation overhead [49]. This cost limits the number of recursive steps that can be
    83→instantiated in practice.
    84→ A common optimization, used in ProtoGalaxy [30] and in PCD solutions [18,50],
    85→is to process multiple predicate instances or accumulators per recursive step. We
    86→refer to these as multiple accumulation schemes of arity ℓ, following prior termi-
    87→nology [41,23]. This reduces the number of recursive steps by a factor of roughly ℓ,
    88→but it does not necessarily reduce the dominant cost inside each recursive step.
    89→4
    90→ In practice, the recursion overhead also encompasses auxiliary operations such as
    91→ hashing of instances.

    99→ The core issue is that recursion overhead is often dominated by commitment
   100→random linear combination (CRC) operations, which are circuit-inefficient. For
   101→curve-based commitments, CRC typically includes non-native elliptic-curve oper-
   102→ations. For code-based commitments, CRC typically includes Merkle path verifi-
   103→cation and related hashing. Existing multiple accumulation schemes [19,18,12,42,23]
   104→require the verifier to perform Θ(ℓ) CRC operations per step.
   105→ Formally, let CRC denote the cost of computing one random linear combina-
   106→tion C := r1 · C1 + r2 · C2 . To prove N computation chunks, an IVC protocol
   107→employing a 2-accumulation (left of Figure 1) requires N steps and a total of
   108→N · t CRC operations in the recursive circuits, where t is the maximum number of
   109→commitments in an accumulator. Thus, though multiple accumulation (right of
   110→Figure 1) reduces the number of recursive steps, it does not remove the dominant
   111→in-circuit CRC work, since existing ℓ-ary verifiers still incur Θ(ℓ) such operations
   112→per step. In recursive settings where CRC is circuit-expensive, this leaves the
   113→dominant recursion overhead asymptotically unchanged in ℓ.
   114→ These considerations motivate the following question.
   115→
   116→Motivation. Can we design a multiple accumulation scheme whose recursive
   117→verifier uses a constant number of in-circuit CRC operations in the arity ℓ?
   118→Equivalently, can we make the dominant in-circuit commitment-combination
   119→cost scale as O(1) in ℓ, while treating the linear cost of reading and processing
   120→public inputs as orthogonal and unchanged?
   121→
   122→1.2 Contributions
   123→Quasar is a multiple accumulation scheme whose recursive verifier uses a con-
   124→stant number of CRC operations in the arity ℓ. Quasar answers the above question
   125→by keeping the dominant in-circuit commitment-combination cost constant in ℓ.
   126→
   127→New interactive oracle reduction. Building on oracle batching [22], we iden-
   128→tify a recursion-friendly reduction interface—multi-cast—that compresses ℓ in-
   129→
   130→ 3
   131→stances into a constant-commitment derived instance suitable for folding. At a
   132→high level, multi-cast reduction transforms ℓ input instances into a single derived
   133→instance while ensuring that the output instance-proof pair contains only a con-
   134→stant number of polynomial commitments. Technically, Quasar achieves this via
   135→an efficient polynomial partial evaluation check that lets the verifier validate the
   136→required commitment relations with O(1) CRC operations, plus O(log ℓ) field
   137→work. This targets the dominant circuit cost in recursive settings, while leav-
   138→ing the linear cost of reading and processing public inputs unchanged. The key
   139→structural point is that multi-cast outputs a constant-size committed interface
   140→that is preserved under folding, rather than merely providing a batched check
   141→over ℓ instances.
   142→
   143→Accumulation schemes. We propose a framework for constructing accumu-
   144→lation schemes from (i) a multi-cast reduction and (ii) a 2-to-1 reduction for
   145→special-sound protocols (SPS) [16]. Main Theorem 1 formalizes the composition
   146→and isolates the ℓ-dependence to O(log ℓ) explicit field work, while CRC remains
   147→independent of ℓ. For simplicity, we focus on the setting where each recursive
   148→step takes ℓ NARK (Non-interactive ARguments of Knowledge) proofs and one
   149→running accumulator as inputs. In this setting, each fold level uses O(1) CRC
   150→operations and the O(log ℓ) levels contribute O(log ℓ) explicit field work, ex-
   151→cluding linear public-input processing. Since our construction is phrased over
   152→polynomial oracles, it is compatible with a variety of polynomial commitments,
   153→and can achieve plausible post-quantum security based on cryptographic hash
   154→functions or lattice-based assumptions [22,11].
   155→
   156→Instantiations and applications. To demonstrate applicability, we construct
   157→a multi-instance IVC protocol that, at each step, takes ℓ predicate instances and
   158→a single running accumulator as inputs. We instantiate the underlying polyno-
   159→mial commitment scheme (PCS) using linear-time encodable codes [35,56], and
   160→employ a NARK for multivariate Plonkish constraint systems [24], yielding a
   161→post-quantum IVC with a linear-time prover. Although this multi-instance IVC
   162→does not have the tree structure of PCD [50], it enables parallel execution of the
   163→accumulation prover within each step. Applying the same technique to PCD can
   164→reduce the recursion costs by a constant factor; however, achieving a PCD with
   165→an accumulation verifier that is sublinear in ℓ in the strong sense remains open.

   168→We analyze the recursive verification cost by separating (i) in-circuit commit-

   170→accumulation arity. We write CRC for one in-circuit commitment random linear
   171→combination, e.g., computing C := r1 · C1 + r2 · C2 . In curve-based instantiations,
   172→CRC induces non-native elliptic-curve arithmetic inside the recursive circuit. In

   174→cation. Quasar reduces the in-circuit CRC cost from Θ(ℓ) to O(1) per recursive

   202→1.4 Scope
   203→
   204→Although folding/accumulation schemes enable practical IVC/PCD, key deploy-
   205→ment requirements remain orthogonal to the bottleneck studied here. First,
   206→zkVM-style workloads (e.g., EVM, RISC-V, Wasm) often yield heterogeneous
   207→circuits, whereas many recursive systems are optimized for more uniform con-
   208→straint structures. Second, many IVC/PCD constructions target a single prover
   209→and do not provide per-step zero-knowledge for intermediate witnesses (e.g.,
   210→Nova [42]), limiting use in privacy-sensitive settings.
   211→ Our goal is to introduce a clean primitive (multi-cast reduction) and a mod-
   212→ular composition theorem that yields O(1) CRC per recursive step. Accordingly,
   213→our evaluation is scoped to validating the circuit footprint implied by the cost
   214→model, rather than end-to-end system throughput. In particular, we measure
   215→constraint counts (and related resource usage) for the accumulation-verification
   216→logic embedded in a recursive circuit under varying arity, and we treat these
   217→results as sanity-check datapoints for the predicted ℓ-dependence. We do not
   218→report end-to-end multi-instance IVC performance, since a complete prototype
   219→would require a recursive wrapper together with an in-circuit verifier for each
   220→PCS backend, and these engineering components are orthogonal to the primi-
   221→tive and composition results established here. Concrete wall-clock performance

   254→2 Technical Overview
   255→We summarize the recursive-accumulation bottleneck and how Quasar removes
   256→it. The core idea is to use polynomial partial evaluation so that ℓ instances can
   257→be mixed while committing to only O(1) objects. We then present Quasar as the
   258→composition of two primitives, a multi-cast reduction and a 2-to-1 reduction,
   259→which together yield a multi-instance accumulation scheme. Finally, we outline
   260→the resulting multi-instance IVC application.
   261→
   262→2.1 New Accumulation Idea
   263→We begin by recalling the accumulation bottleneck. An accumulation scheme
   264→incrementally aggregates NP statements into a succinct accumulator such that
   265→each step is efficiently verifiable. Most practical constructions [14,19,18,12,42,16]
   266→rely on homomorphic commitments, which force the verifier to compute random
   267→linear combinations of commitments. In recursive settings, these commitment
   268→random linear combination (CRC) operations are typically circuit-inefficient.

   340→2.2 Main Result: Multi-instance Accumulation with Constant CRC
   341→
   342→We adopt the accumulation framework of Bünz et al. [22,17], which builds accu-
   343→mulation schemes from interactive oracle reductions (IOR; see Section 3.3). At a
   344→high level, the framework uses:
   345→
   346→• a cast IOR from an NP relation R to a relation Racc , and
   347→• a many-to-one IOR from (Racc )ℓ to Racc .
   348→
   349→The bottleneck in prior multi-instance designs is that the many-to-one step com-
   350→bines ℓ committed objects, forcing Θ(ℓ) in-circuit CRC operations in the recursive
   351→verifier. Quasar shifts this combination from the folding stage to a cast stage that
   352→takes non-committed inputs, so that the recursive verifier pays only a constant
   353→number of CRC operations.
   354→ To better accomodate to existing proof systems, we follow Protostar [16]
   355→and adopt our accumulation to special-sound protocols (SPS; see Section 5.1).
   356→The result is formalized as Main Theorem 1. We measure recursive-verifier cost
   357→by separating (i) circuit-expensive CRC and (ii) standard processing of public
   358→inputs. A CRC is one in-circuit operation computing a random linear combination
   359→of two committed objects, e.g., C := r1 ·C1 +r2 ·C2 . All bounds below exclude the
   360→unavoidable linear cost of reading and processing the ℓ public instances, which
   361→remains linear as usual.
   362→Theorem 1 (Composing multi-cast with 2-to-1 folding). Let ΠSPS be
   363→a special-sound protocol for a relation R. Assume we are given: (i) a multi-cast
   364→reduction IORcast-SPS (and its Fiat–Shamir compilation NIRcast-SPS ), and (ii) a 2-
   365→to-1 folding reduction IORfold-SPS (and its Fiat–Shamir compilation NIRfold-SPS ),
   366→both instantiated with an extractable PCS and compiled in the random-oracle
   367→model. Then the construction in Section 6 yields: (a) a non-interactive argument
   368→
   369→ 8
   370→NARK for ℓ statements of R, and (b) a multi-instance accumulation scheme ACC
   371→for NARK, satisfying perfect completeness and round-by-round (RBR) knowledge
   372→soundness in the random-oracle model. Moreover, each fold level invokes the un-
   373→derlying batching verifier a constant number of times (independent of ℓ), and the
   374→O(log ℓ) fold levels contribute only explicit field work (e.g., sum-check / partial
   375→evaluation), excluding linear public-input processing. (Each batching invocation
   376→combines two committed oracles into one; see Fig. 11.)
   377→
   378→Proof idea. The multi-cast reduction compresses ℓ input instances into a single
   379→accumulator instance while keeping the accumulator interface constant-size (a
   380→constant number of oracle polynomials). The folding reduction then combines
   381→two such accumulator instances into one without increasing that interface, since
   382→it only requires a constant number of batching checks. Therefore, the number
   383→of commitment-combination checks in the recursive circuit is independent of ℓ,
   384→while the only ℓ-dependent work introduced by multi-cast is explicit field work
   385→(sum-check / partial-evaluation), and linear public-input processing is orthogo-
   386→nal and excluded from the metric.
   387→
   388→Why this is not immediate. It is well-known that sum-check and partial-evaluation
   389→tests can certify polynomial identities and batched evaluation claims. In partic-
   390→ular, prior multi-instance designs typically incur Θ(ℓ) commitment-combination
   391→work per step in the recursive verifier, even when their arithmetic can be orga-
   392→nized as an MSM. Our main contribution is not the existence of these checks, but
   393→an accumulation interface that makes them compatible with recursion at arity
   394→ℓ. Concretely, a naı̈ve “batch ℓ instances and sum-check” approach still leaves
   395→the recursive verifier facing Θ(ℓ) committed objects (or Θ(ℓ) commitment com-
   396→binations) somewhere in the pipeline, so the in-circuit commitment-combination
   397→cost does not disappear. Quasar’s multi-cast reduction is designed to output
   398→a constant-size committed instance (a constant number of oracle polynomials)
   399→whose verification requires only O(1) in-circuit CRC, independent of ℓ. The com-
   400→position theorem then shows that this constant-size interface is preserved under
   401→2-to-1 folding, so Θ(ℓ) committed objects do not reappear across recursive steps.
   402→This interface-preservation property, together with a clean cost separation that
   403→excludes linear public-input processing, is the structural novelty that yields con-
   404→stant in-circuit CRC in ℓ. We further illustrate this framework in Figure 2.
