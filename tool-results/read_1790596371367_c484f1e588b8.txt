     9→ Abstract. The state-of-the-art lattice-based fully-succinct argument framework of “RoK, paper, SISsors
    10→ (RPS)” and “RoK and Roll (RnR)” [ASIACRYPT’24,’25] comprises a suite of atomically composable
    11→ reductions of knowledge (RoK) for bounded-norm tensor-structured linear equations. While the framework
    12→ can be used to build polynomial commitment schemes (PCS), hence succinct non-interactive arguments
    13→ of knowledge (SNARK), the bounded norm guarantee is abstracted away from higher-level applications.
    14→ Furthermore, RPS/RnR’s lazy batching RoK only reduces the proof size but not time, and their
    15→ (ℓ2 -)norm-check RoK has a Oλ (m log m)-time prover, a concrete bottleneck in applications.
    16→ We present SALSAA, which incorporates the sumcheck protocol into the RPS/RnR framework to addi-
    17→ tionally support verifying sumcheck claims over bounded-norm witnesses of structured linear relations.
    18→ This grants the flexibility of sumchecks, exposes the norm guarantee to higher-level applications and,
    19→ perhaps counter-intuitively, leads to efficiency improvements in the framework itself. We use SALSAA
    20→ to construct the following: (i) An improved (ℓ2 -)norm-check RoK with an Oλ (m)-time prover, and an
    21→ improved batching RoK that also reduces prover time. These combine to yield a concretely efficient
    22→ SNARK for structured linear relations and a PCS, with half the proof size of RPS and verification in
    23→ under 7 ms for witnesses of up to 230 Zq elements. (ii) Improved RoKs for checking binariness and
    24→ staircase-structured linear relations, which in turn yield a more practical lattice-based verifiable delay
    25→ function (VDF). (iii) A folding scheme for bounded-ℓ2 -norm (semi-)structured linear relations, with
    26→ sub-millisecond verification and proofs of roughly 60 KB per folding step. (iv) A RoK for the Algebraic
    27→ Intermediate Representation (AIR) relation which, through (i) and (iii), yields a lattice-based succinct
    28→ argument and a folding scheme for AIR.
    29→ Finally, we provide modular implementations of (i)–(iii) in one codebase relying on AVX-512-accelerated
    30→ cyclotomic ring arithmetic.
    31→
    32→ Keywords: post-quantum cryptography, lattice-based cryptography, succinct arguments, sumcheck,
    33→ folding schemes, verifiable delay functions

    35→1 Introduction
    36→
    37→Succinct argument systems [Kil92,Mic94] are cryptographic protocols that enable a prover to convince a
    38→verifier of the validity of a mathematical statement with communication much shorter than the size of the
    39→statement. Unlike group-based constructions (e.g. [PHGR13,Gro16,BBB+ 18,CHM+ 20,KZG10]), lattice-based
    40→argument systems promise post-quantum security and exploit rich algebraic structures that may lead to greater
    41→efficiency and expressiveness compared to hash-based approaches (e.g. [BBHR18,BCR+ 19,GLS+ 23,AHIV23]).
    42→Furthermore, lattice-based succinct arguments can be used to natively prove relations that arise from lattice-
    43→based cryptographic constructions, which are predominant in the post-quantum setting, without requiring
    44→translations between mathematical structures that induce extra overhead. This work contributes to the
    45→ongoing effort to realise the latter potential in practice.
    46→ Succinct non-interactive arguments of knowledge (SNARKs) are a central component in application
    47→scenarios requiring efficient verification, such as verifiable delegation of computation [KP16,Lee21,GSW23],
    48→verifiable fully homomorphic encryption [GBK+ 24,ABPS24,LLZ+ 25,ZLW+ 25], aggregate signatures [BK20,
    49→JRS23,JRS24,AAB+ 24,DKLW25], and verifiable delay functions [LM23,OKC+ 25]. Folding schemes [KST22,
    50→BC25a,BC25b,FKNP24,NS25,GLLO26] are closely related to SNARKs and allow a prover to fold multiple
    51→instances of a relation into fewer ones, which leads to constructions of incrementally verifiable computation
    52→(IVC) [Val08] and proof-carrying data (PCD) [CT10].
    53→ 1.1 Lattice-based Succinct Arguments
    54→
    55→Lattice-based succinct arguments can be divided into semi- and fully-succinct systems. Semi-succinct systems
    56→such as LaBRADOR [BS23] and Greyhound [NS24] already achieve polylogarithmic-size proofs, but their ver-
    57→ifier work remains super-logarithmic. Fully-succinct systems [CLM23,AFLN24,CMNW24,KLNO24,KLNO25]
    58→feature polylogarithmic-time verifiers. The frameworks of “RoK, paper, SISsors” (RPS) [KLNO24] and “RoK
    59→and Roll” (RnR) [KLNO25] are the most practically relevant in the fully-succinct line.
    60→ The RPS/RnR line of work provides a modular framework, described in the language of reductions of
    61→knowledge (RoKs), for constructing succinct arguments. A RoK generalises a traditional proof system: It
    62→allows a prover and a verifier to interactively reduce an instance of a starting relation to an instance of an
    63→often simpler target relation. The RPS/RnR framework in particular provides RoKs between bounded-norm
    64→structured linear relations Ξ lin-⊗ with different parameters. For example, folding reduces the witness dimension,
    65→norm checking reduces an exact norm claim into a relaxed norm claim, and batching reduces the number of
    66→constraints. These atomic RoKs compose into succinct arguments with different trade-offs, which can further
    67→be turned non-interactive using the Fiat-Shamir transform. The modularity makes it possible to examine and
    68→remove individual bottlenecks without rebuilding the rest of the framework.
    69→ The RPS/RnR framework suffers from one main bottleneck: a lack of expressiveness. The only supported
    70→relation is the structured linear relation Ξ lin-⊗ , which captures inner products between the witness and public
    71→vectors, where the public vectors must be tensor-structured. Although a SNARK for Ξ lin-⊗ immediately
    72→gives a polynomial commitment scheme (PCS), through which one can compile polynomial interactive
    73→oracle proofs (PIOP) to obtain SNARKs for various expressive relations, such as Algebraic Intermediate
    74→Representations (AIR) or Rank-1 Constraint Systems (R1CS), the compilation abstracts away the norm bound
    75→guarantee of the RPS/RnR framework which is potentially useful for higher-level applications. Indeed, relations
    76→expressed as bounded-norm satisfiability of polynomial equations naturally arise in lattice-based cryptographic
    77→constructions, such as verifiable FHE, proof of correct encryption/decryption or aggregating lattice-based
    78→signatures. Expressing these bounded norm constraints via AIR/R1CS/etc. would be highly inefficient. This
    79→overhead is in principle unnecessary if the lattice-based RPS/RnR framework is used as the backend.
    80→ Somewhat counter-intuitively, the limitation also affects the internals of the framework. In particular,
    81→the norm-check RoK of RPS/RnR is a key component for solving the soundness gap problem common in
    82→lattice-based arguments. Since only a linear Ξ lin-⊗ interface is exposed, the norm check is proven via an
    83→auxiliary commitment to the convolution of the witness with itself, which takes Oλ (m log m) ring operations
    84→for a m-dimensional witness and dominates the prover’s cost. This cost is inherited by applications built on
    85→the framework, such as the verifiable delay function Papercraft [OKC+ 25].
    86→
    87→ 1.2 Our Contributions
    88→
    89→We identify the above as an interfacing problem between the RPS/RnR framework and higher-level applications.
    90→To solve it, we extend the framework to expose a compatible sumcheck relation Ξ sum as a reduction target for
    91→other RoKs, by integrating the machinery of the sumcheck protocol [LFKN90]. An Ξ sum instance is simply
    92→an Ξ lin-⊗ instance together with a list of sumcheck claims, which can encode a wide range of non-linear
    93→relations. The technical core of our work is a sumcheck RoK that reduces Ξ sum to a multilinear extension
    94→(MLE) relation Ξ mle , which in turn reduces to Ξ lin-⊗ , all with the prover performing Oλ (m) ring operations.
    95→Our sumcheck is run directly over cyclotomic ring elements rather than over their coefficients, which avoids
    96→the large modulus or extension field that a coefficient-wise sumcheck [BC25a,NS25,GLLO26] would require.
    97→Equipped with a RoK from Ξ sum to Ξ lin-⊗ , we obtain a suite of new RoKs from various relations to Ξ lin-⊗ :
    98→
    99→ (i) An improved norm-check RoK with a Oλ (m)-time prover in ring operations. It is a drop-in replacement
   100→ for the Oλ (m log m)-time norm-check of RPS [KLNO24].
   101→ (ii) An improved batching RoK, which compresses the many constraints of a Ξ lin-⊗ instance into a single one.
   102→ This improves upon the “lazy batching” RoK of [KLNO24] which reduces only the proof size but not the
   103→ prover time.
   104→(iii) A binariness RoK, which certifies that the witness of a Ξ lin-⊗ instance is binary. This replaces the binariness
   105→ RoK in RPS/Papercraft which inherits the Oλ (m log m) time cost from the norm-check of RPS and which
   106→ requires the witness to live in a “tensor subring”.
   107→
   108→ 2
   109→ (iv) A staircase RoK, which supports constraints represented by block-bidiagonal matrices, i.e. two blocks
   110→ repeating along the diagonal, as arises when the witness is the trace of a computation repeating the same
   111→ step. Compared with its analogue in Papercraft, our staircase RoK is more flexible in that it decouples the
   112→ block width of the block-bidiagonal matrix from the tensor-structure of the underlying Ξ lin-⊗ instance.
   113→ (v) An “Algebraic Intermediate Representation” (AIR [BBHR19,Sta21]) RoK, which asserts that a Ξ lin-⊗
   114→ witness satisfies some given AIR arithmetisation constraints. There, the witness is the execution trace of
   115→ a computation, a low-degree transition function constrains every pair of consecutive rows of the trace,
   116→ and boundary conditions fix the initial and final states. This RoK interfaces the framework with the
   117→ arithmetisation emitted by standard SNARK front ends.
   118→

   120→ steps being shared by all of them:

   123→ {Ξ lin-⊗ , Ξ bin , Ξ , Ξ com-air } −→ Ξ sum −→ Ξ mle −→ Ξ lin-⊗ .
   124→
   125→ By virtue of the modularity of the RPS/RnR framework, composing the above new RoKs enables new
   126→ applications. We construct some of them below:
   127→
   128→ (vi) Improved SNARKs for the structured linear relation Ξ lin-⊗ , which immediately gives improved polynomial
   129→ commitment schemes (PCS). The PCS is immediate because a commitment to the witness doubles as
   130→ a commitment to its multilinear extension, so an evaluation claim is an instance of Ξ mle . These take
   131→ advantage of the new norm-check and batching RoKs.
   132→ (vii) Improved verifiable delay functions (VDF), following the blueprint of [OKC+ 25]. In brief, the execution
   133→ trace of the delay function can be expressed as the binary-satisfiability of a structured linear equation
   134→ represented by a block-bidiagonal matrix. The improved staircase and binariness RoKs reduce this to
   135→ Ξ lin-⊗ where the SNARK in item (vi) applies.
   136→(viii) Folding schemes for bounded-ℓ2 -norm semi-structured linear relation Ξ lin- , which differs from Ξ lin-⊗ in
   137→ that it admits unstructured commitment keys.
   138→ (ix) By composing the AIR RoK with a SNARK for Ξ lin-⊗ or a folding scheme for Ξ lin- , we obtain a
   139→ SNARK/folding scheme for AIR, respectively.
   140→
   141→ To demonstrate practicality, we implement applications (vi)–(viii) above and report their concrete performance
   142→ in Section 8. For instance, our implementations offer the most efficient verification of any reported lattice-
   143→ based SNARK construction to our knowledge, at 5.64 ms for a witness of 228 Zq elements, even faster than
   144→ RoKoko [KLN+ 26], which in exchange achieves smaller proofs and a faster prover. The VDF verifies in roughly
   145→ 10 ms, two to three orders of magnitude below the delay itself for considered parameters, while proving costs
   146→ a constant multiple of the delay. One folding step verifies in under half a millisecond and communicates about
   147→ 60 KB, independently of the witness size.

   149→ 1.3 Related Work
   150→
   151→ The line of works most relevant to SALSAA is initiated in [CLM23] and refined in RPS [KLNO24] and
   152→ RnR [KLNO25]. These schemes rely on the vSIS assumption, a structured variant of standard SIS, to achieve
   153→ polylogarithmic verifier time.
   154→ The sumcheck protocol [LFKN90] is by now a standard tool for constructing succinct arguments [CHM+ 20,
   155→ Set20,CBBZ23,XZZ+ 19,Che25], especially those obtained by compiling a polynomial interactive oracle proof
   156→ (PIOP) with a polynomial commitment scheme (PCS) [XZZ+ 19,BCG+ 19,GWC19,BFS20,CHM+ 20,Set20,
   157→ BCHO22,CBBZ23]. Our use is different: rather than compiling a standalone PIOP with a polynomial
   158→ commitment scheme, we integrate sumcheck into the RPS/RnR framework, and we run it directly over
   159→ cyclotomic ring elements rather than over their coefficients. Such a sumcheck is more concretely efficient
   160→ and yields a similar communication cost thanks to batching over NTT (number-theoretic transform) slots as
   161→ observed in [BC25b].
   162→ For lattice-based folding schemes, a linear-time prover and expressiveness are typically obtained via
   163→ sumchecks [BC25a,BC25b,NS25,GLLO26]. In none of these works, however, is the sumcheck exposed as a
   164→ relation in its own right that other reductions can target: it is applied, in a rather ad hoc manner, inside a
   165→
   166→ 3
   167→monolithic protocol for one fixed arithmetisation. Most of these works also share a common template: first
   168→reduce a CCS/R1CS instance to a linear relation, then fold several instances of that linear relation into fewer
   169→ones.1 We contribute to both steps. For the first, our AIR RoK reduces AIR constraints to Ξ lin directly, without
   170→routing them through CCS. For the second, we build an efficient folding scheme for Ξ lin itself by composing
   171→our improved norm-check and batching RoKs with those of RPS/RnR.
   172→ The Algebraic Intermediate Representation (AIR) [BBHR19] originated from the hash-based STARK
   173→line [BBHR18,Sta21]. AIR is particularly useful when verifier succinctness is a priority, as the instance descrip-
   174→tion is as small as the description of the transition function. Other popular arithmetisation languages include
   175→rank-1 constraint systems (R1CS) [GGPR13,BCG+ 13], Plonkish arithmetisation [GWC19], and customisable
   176→constraint systems (CCS) [STW23], the last of which can be instantiated to recover R1CS, Plonkish and AIR
   177→as special cases. In R1CS and CCS the dimensions of the constraint matrices are as large as the computation
   178→description, although the matrices are typically sparse. Proof systems that do not exploit this sparsity typically
   179→require the verifier to preprocess the matrices to achieve succinct verification.
   180→ Lattice-based arguments and folding schemes that support a general arithmetisation have so far targeted
   181→CCS or R1CS [BC25a,BC25b,NS25,GLLO26], which leaves AIR constraints reachable only by embedding AIR
   182→into CCS, reintroducing the large matrices and the verifier cost that AIR avoids. To our knowledge, our AIR
   183→RoK is the first direct treatment of AIR constraints in a lattice-based argument system.
   184→
   185→1.4 Subsequent Work
   186→
   187→The subsequent work RoKoko [KLN+ 26] adopts and extends our integration of sumcheck into the RPS/RnR
   188→framework: each round alternates between generalisations of Ξ lin-⊗ and Ξ sum , where the latter expresses all
   189→constraints–linear constraints, well-formedness of recursive commitments, exact norm check, etc.–as sumcheck
   190→claims. While SALSAA optimises verification time, RoKoko trades a slight increase in verification for a significant
   191→reduction in proof size and prover time. Achieving the best of both is an interesting open problem. Furthermore,
   192→while RoKoko focuses on improving the underlying argument system, a core part of SALSAA is to interface
   193→various applications, which is transferable to RoKoko.
