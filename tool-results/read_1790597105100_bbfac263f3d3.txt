  2604→ 8.1 Design Choices and Experimental Setup
  2605→ For the most optimal performance, we choose to work with power-of-two cyclotomic rings R = Z[ζf ] where
  2606→ f = 2k for some integer k. Power-of-two cyclotomic rings admit efficient arithmetic using the Number-Theoretic
  2607→ Transform (NTT). We assume that the cyclotomic ring “almost splits” modulo q, i.e. Rq ∼ = (Fq2 )φ/2 . Using
  2608→ this ring allows us to use cyclotomic ring elements with binary coefficients as challenges for Π fold by applying
  2609→ the analysis from [GLLO26, appendix B.2]. Using “almost-splitting” cyclotomic rings allows us to use Fqe
  2610→ (with e = 2) as a challenge set for sumcheck and conveniently batch across the φ/e CRT/NTT slots. We
  2611→ select modulus q ≈ 250 so that the operations’ runtime is further improved using AVX-512-IFMA instructions
  2612→ available on modern Intel processors. We operate over the f-th cyclotomic ring for f = 256 (i.e. φ = 128),
  2613→ observing that the higher degree typically implies better performance, in exchange for larger proof sizes.
  2614→
  2615→ Ring arithmetic. Our implementation of SALSAA relies heavily on efficient cyclotomic ring arithmetic, which
  2616→ is crucial for the performance of our protocols. We rely on the incomplete-rexl3 library developed for
  2617→ efficient multiplication in “almost-splitting” cyclotomic rings. It provides a Rust implementation of a subset
  2618→ of operations supported by Intel HEXL [BKS+ 21] and is likewise optimised for the AVX-512 instruction set.

  2767→size is essentially independent of the witness size, while its verification remains slower than ours throughout.
  2768→Greyhound shows the semi-succinct profile, with verification time growing with the square root of the witness
  2769→size. Among all baselines only WHIR verifies faster, at the price of a prover several times slower than ours
  2770→and, as for all hash-based systems, without native support for bounded-norm lattice statements.
  2771→ Our VDF prover runs at a constant multiple of the delay itself, roughly 80 including commitment, as both
  2772→are linear in the input, and verification never exceeds half a percent of the delay. The per-step delay cost is
  2773→constant across sizes, so the delay grows purely through the step count. The nominal ratio of Papercraft is
  2774→lower only because it is measured against its five times slower delay steps. Normalised to our per-step cost it
  2775→is close to three times ours, its verification is slower by more than two orders of magnitude, and its proofs are
  2776→larger by more than one.
  2777→ For the folding scheme, verification time and proof size are essentially independent of the witness size: the
  2778→accumulator and the folding messages have fixed dimensions, and only the sumcheck rounds contribute a
  2779→logarithmic term. The folding prover is linear in the witness and costs about a seventh of the SNARK prover
  2780→at equal size, since one folding step runs a constant number of RoKs rather than the full composition.
